# Chapter 10 Research Pass

## Provisional title

**Sovereign and Public Capital — Grants, Tax Expenditures, Public Loans, Utility
Investment, Municipal Finance, and National Champions**

## Research question

Which AI assets are financed or de-risked by taxpayers, public lenders, regulated
ratepayers, municipal issuers, sovereign wealth, public pension or insurance capital,
and national industrial-policy vehicles—and what rights, milestones, recapture
provisions or residual risks does the public obtain in return?

## Pass 1 scope

Chapter 10 follows public capital through seven distinct channels:

1. direct grants and cooperative agreements;
2. public loans, loan guarantees and credit support;
3. investment and production tax credits, elective payments and accelerated
   depreciation;
4. government equity, warrants or other contingent-value rights;
5. state and local tax abatements, infrastructure grants and land arrangements;
6. municipal, federal and regulated-utility infrastructure financing;
7. sovereign wealth, national champions and cross-border industrial-policy capital.

The chapter will not define every announced data center, semiconductor fab or utility
project as an AI subsidy. AI attribution requires a documented role in accelerator,
memory, packaging, model-training, data-center, network or power capacity used for AI.

## Required reconstruction bridges

1. **Award bridge:** authorized program → preliminary term sheet → final award →
   milestone → obligation → disbursement → expenditure → placed-in-service output.
2. **Tax bridge:** statutory rate → qualified basis → placed-in-service date →
   taxpayer election → credit recognized or paid → basis interaction → recapture.
3. **Loan bridge:** public authority → committed loan → draw → project expenditure →
   repayment → subsidy cost or loss.
4. **Equity bridge:** public cash → shares or warrants → voting and economic rights →
   mark → sale or retained public value.
5. **Infrastructure bridge:** public or regulated utility asset → dedicated versus
   shared benefit → cost allocation → customer contribution → ratepayer or taxpayer
   residual risk.
6. **Sovereign bridge:** state capital → fund or national champion → company / project
   investment → domestic-capacity obligation → cross-border dependency.
7. **Attribution bridge:** public amount → eligible project costs → AI-relevant
   physical output → beneficiary and ultimate economic user.

## Non-negotiable measurement rules

- Statutory authorization is not an award.
- A preliminary memorandum of terms is not a final award.
- A final award ceiling is not cash disbursed.
- A government loan is not a grant and is not necessarily a subsidy equal to
  principal.
- A tax-credit rate is not a cash amount without qualified basis and placed-in-service
  evidence.
- A tax credit and direct grant may apply to overlapping project cost and cannot be
  added without the coordination rules.
- “Private investment catalyzed” is not public expenditure or documented causal
  attribution.
- A public disbursement exchanged for shares is government equity rather than a
  conventional nonrecoverable grant at that point in time.
- A municipal-utility asset is not automatically taxpayer-subsidized; it may be
  customer-funded, debt-financed, rate-based or federally supplied.
- A tax abatement is foregone revenue over a schedule, not cash paid at announcement.
- A sovereign fund commitment is not a national fiscal expenditure until the legal
  holder, funding source and status are known.
- Government ownership does not prove control over commercial operations.
- Public support for a multi-product fab is not wholly attributable to AI.

## Existing evidence to carry forward

- Canada finalized an up-to-$240 million contribution to a $725 million Cohere compute
  project in which Cohere is anchor tenant and CoreWeave builds and operates the
  facility. The public record does not allocate the remaining $485 million among
  customer, operator, lenders or other capital.
- MLGW reported customer-funded substations and transmission upgrades for xAI's Lowry
  site. Municipal ownership of the utility does not mean ordinary customers funded
  the dedicated assets.
- Entergy Louisiana's Meta-linked projects show a different model: regulated utility
  investment, customer contribution, minimum billing and commission-approved cost
  allocation must be separated.
- Micron, SK hynix and Samsung filings identify public incentives as part of their
  capacity stacks, but Chapter 8 did not reconstruct award, tax-credit, disbursement
  and output states.
- EMMA and USAspending are registered discovery systems; neither substitutes for the
  official statement, award agreement, tax return disclosure or project filing.

## Pass 1A — CHIPS awards are milestone-contingent capital rather than headline cash

The initial primary-source inventory identifies several final award ceilings:

- TSMC Arizona: up to $6.6 billion of direct funding and up to $5 billion of proposed
  loans supporting a stated investment of more than $65 billion.
- Micron: up to $6.165 billion of direct funding for the Idaho and New York program
  within a much longer and larger company investment vision.
- Samsung: up to $4.745 billion of direct funding supporting a stated investment of
  more than $37 billion.
- Intel: up to $7.865 billion of direct funding across projects in Arizona, New
  Mexico, Ohio and Oregon.
- SK hynix: up to $458 million of direct funding and up to $500 million of loans for
  an approximately $3.87 billion Indiana HBM and packaging project.

These are not additive to stated private project values. Commerce states that direct
funds are disbursed against construction, production and commercial milestones and
that loans are disbursed against invested capital expenditure. Each company therefore
requires an award-to-disbursement ledger rather than an award announcement table.

## Pass 1B — Intel's award changed legal form into government equity

The Department of Commerce fiscal 2025 Agency Financial Report records a material
post-award restructuring. In August 2025, NIST paid Intel $5.70 billion of accelerated
disbursements and received 274,583,000 Intel common shares at $20.74 per share.
Separately, a $13.7 million Department of War Secure Enclave disbursement produced
684,309 shares at $20 per share.

This is not accurately described as an ordinary grant after conversion:

`public cash → Intel common equity → market value and sale / retention risk`

The transaction may preserve upside or expose the government to share-price loss. The
research pass must reconstruct the amended agreement, voting and disposal terms,
accounting classification, later holdings and whether the equity replaced, accelerated
or conditioned previously awarded direct funding.

## Pass 1C — The tax-credit rate changed across placed-in-service dates

The original section 48D advanced-manufacturing investment credit was 25% of qualified
investment. Public Law 119-21 amended the percentage to 35% for property placed in
service after 31 December 2025. Current IRS Form 3468 instructions reflect the
date-dependent rates.

The critical variable is not announcement or construction start alone. It is qualified
basis placed in service in the relevant taxable year. The chapter must reconcile:

`project announcement ≠ construction cost ≠ qualified basis ≠ placed-in-service basis
≠ credit claimed ≠ elective cash payment`

It must also test direct-award coordination, basis reduction, foreign-country-of-
concern recapture and any company-disclosed receivable or tax benefit.

## Pass 1D — Public infrastructure uses different cost-allocation models

Chapter 5 established that the same public-looking power system can allocate costs
differently:

- xAI's MLGW expansion uses customer-funded dedicated substations and transmission
  work plus curtailment;
- an investor-owned regulated utility may own and rate-base shared generation or
  transmission while a large-load contract imposes contributions and minimum bills;
- federal power entities can supply wholesale power or transmission without owning
  the data center;
- public infrastructure investment can have residual benefits or costs outside the
  named customer.

Chapter 10 must identify issuer, debt holder, contributed asset, depreciation,
customer payment, ratepayer allocation and public guarantee before describing any
utility investment as subsidy.

## Observed-fact boundary

Observed facts include executed awards, filed cash receipts, recognized tax credits,
issued or escrowed securities, ownership statements, regulatory cost allocations and
official sovereign-vehicle creation. Targets, mobilization goals and stated enterprise
values remain observations only of what the source says; they are not recast as
funded public capital.

## Reconstruction boundary

The reconstruction may connect:

`authorization → final award → milestone → cash → accounting → physical output`

only where each bridge is evidenced. It may also reconcile government cash to equity
or utility investment to the ultimate residual holder. It may not infer a cash draw
from a final award or infer sovereign funding from government ownership.

## Interpretation boundary

Interpretation begins when the chapter assesses:

- whether public capital reduces private capital requirements;
- whether taxpayers retain repayment or equity value;
- whether an incentive changes after-tax project return;
- whether dedicated infrastructure is customer-funded;
- whether a shared utility asset leaves post-contract ratepayer or utility risk;
- whether a sovereign vehicle creates control before its capital is measurable.

Those assessments do not change the evidence grade of the underlying observations.

## Evidence that would overturn the synthesis

The chapter's conclusions would materially change if later primary evidence shows:

1. TSMC Arizona, Samsung or SK hynix award ceilings were already fully disbursed by
   the research cutoff;
2. CHIPS government loans were drawn on terms that transfer substantially more credit
   subsidy or loss risk than principal accounting indicates;
3. Intel's government shares or warrants are offset by undisclosed hedges, mandatory
   repurchase rights or loss protection;
4. recipient-level section 48D returns show materially smaller qualified basis or cash
   realization than issuer recognition implies;
5. MGX, HUMAIN or InvestAI audited statements identify legally committed and funded
   sovereign capital materially different from the null amounts retained here;
6. utility agreements allocate redacted post-term costs back to the named customer
   rather than the utility or other customers;
7. the remaining C$485 million Cohere project funding is guaranteed or directly
   supplied by government rather than by private counterparties.

Each item is a testable revision trigger rather than a prediction that the evidence
exists.

## Confidence evolution baseline

## Economic-importance summary

The principal **high/immediate** discoveries are the Intel grant-to-equity conversion,
actual Intel and Micron liquidity, Intel tax-credit recognition and the closed Aligned
transaction. The **high/structural** discoveries are milestone-contingent award
funding, section 48D's effect on after-tax capital cost, parent recourse, utility tail
risk and the nonadditive measurement rules required to prevent a false public-capital
total. HUMAIN, MGX and InvestAI ownership or policy announcements are economically
important but remain **medium/optional or structural** until funded capital and
project instruments are disclosed.
