# Monograph Research Plan

The sequence follows capital and risk transmission rather than company categories.
Titles after Chapter 1 remain provisional until their research passes are complete.

1. **Methodology — Reconstructing a system that is not reported as a system**
2. **The capital stack — Who funds AI, through which instruments, and with whose balance sheet**
3. **Compute commitments — GPUs, cloud minimums, prepayments, and capacity reservations**
4. **The data-center financing machine — Developers, hyperscalers, SPVs, lenders, and landlords**
5. **Power — Generation, transmission, special contracts, cost allocation, and public subsidy**
6. **Frontier laboratories — OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, and the economics hidden by private status**
7. **Hyperscaler balance sheets — Capex, leases, purchase commitments, and customer concentration**
8. **Semiconductor control — Nvidia, AMD, foundries, memory, networking, and supplier finance**
9. **Private credit and structured finance — Where leverage leaves public-company accounts**
10. **Sovereign and public capital — Grants, tax incentives, municipal debt, and national champions**
11. **Circularity and cross-ownership — When investor, supplier, customer, and creditor converge**
12. **The economic engine — Price, utilization, useful life, depreciation, variable cost, financing cost, and required return**
13. **Cash waterfalls and return on invested capital — Who is paid, who loses first, and whether the asset earns its cost of capital**
14. **Utilization and cost curves — Reserved versus occupied compute, effective revenue hours, inference-price compression, and survivability**
15. **Control — Governance rights, exclusivity, collateral, step-in rights, and platform dependence**
16. **Counterparty concentration — Centrality, guarantee concentration, customer concentration, and single-point failures**
17. **Stress propagation and system dynamics — Transaction-level shocks, lags, mitigants, and feedback loops**
18. **Historical analogues — Railroads, fiber, independent and merchant power, solar, shipping, cloud, and semiconductors**
19. **What remains unknowable — Disclosure dark matter, falsifiers, confidence evolution, and the monitoring architecture**

## Post-chapter synthesis deliverables

After Chapters 1–19 pass their publication revisions:

Both artifacts are governed by `synthesis_deliverables_standard.md`.
The executive summary, complete monograph, and essay are delivered as three separate
professionally formatted PDFs governed by `publication_pdf_standard.md`.

1. **Executive summary (1–4 pages).** A decision-useful synthesis of the most
   important reconstructed facts, surprises, risk-transfer mechanisms, confidence
   boundaries, and monitoring implications. It must remain traceable to the
   canonical claim and exposure registries.
2. **“Burning Up”-inspired essay for the present AI system.** An original, publication-
   quality analogue to the dot-com break-up article requested by the user. It must
   be derived from this investigation rather than from a generic bubble template;
   distinguish fact, inference, and hypothesis; identify the mechanisms that could
   break, their timing, and their first loss bearers; and explain which apparent
   circularities are real, partial, or unsupported. The essay is a separate
   synthesis artifact, not a substitute for Chapter 18 or Chapter 17.

3. **Complete monograph PDF.** One navigable, book-length publication containing
   Chapters 1–19, consolidated front and back matter, a clickable table of contents,
   bookmarks, cross-references, glossary, source/method notes, and the governed
   monitoring framework. Chapter DOCX files remain separate intermediate
   deliverables and do not replace this assembled publication.
