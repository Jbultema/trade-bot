# Chapters 12–14 Research Program

## Purpose

Chapters 12–14 convert the reconstructed capital, contract and physical system into
testable economics. Research proceeds from shared evidence but produces three distinct
artifacts.

## Chapter 12 — The Economic Engine

### Questions

- What capital and operating cost must a defined AI asset recover?
- Which costs scale with productive use and which persist while idle?
- What utilization and realized price produce operating break-even?
- How do useful life, replacement cadence and public support change required return?
- Which inputs are observed, contract-implied, bounded, scenario-based or unresolved?

### Required datasets

- unit-economics observations;
- pricing observations;
- power, memory, network and other variable costs;
- fixed operating cost;
- maintenance and replacement capital;
- useful-life and depreciation observations;
- tax and public-support adjustments;
- required-return observations by capital class;
- break-even and first-loss threshold outputs.

### Writing gate

At least one project or asset case must contain a fully traceable bounded model. Cases
with missing material inputs remain unresolved and are still reportable discoveries.

## Chapter 13 — Cash Waterfalls and Return on Invested Capital

### Questions

- Who receives project cash and in what priority?
- Which claimant loses first when revenue or value falls?
- Can selected projects earn their financing and equity hurdle rates?
- What accounting and incremental returns can be supported for Microsoft, Amazon,
  Alphabet, Meta and Oracle?
- What revenue, margin or utilization would be required where AI-specific return is
  unobservable?

### Required datasets

- asset-level sources and uses;
- waterfall steps and contractual priorities;
- debt service, amortization, cash sweeps and reserves;
- project IRR models by claimant;
- company accounting ROIC;
- incremental attribution ranges;
- required-revenue models;
- implied-return and reverse-DCF models;
- reconciliation between accounting capital and project capital.

### Writing gate

No company receives one claimed “AI ROIC” unless directly disclosed. Each company must
show which of the five attribution methods is ready, bounded or blocked.

## Chapter 14 — Utilization and Cost Curves

### Questions

- How much installed capacity is available, accepted, reserved, consumed, billable and
  productive?
- What indirect observations can bound utilization without pretending to measure it?
- How quickly are realized compute prices falling?
- How do performance per dollar and watt change economic capacity?
- What useful life and residual value survive technology transitions?
- Which assets survive a 90% inference-price decline and under what cost reductions?

### Triangulation surfaces

- cloud revenue and backlog;
- developer and customer disclosures;
- utility load and interval demand;
- commissioning and acceptance milestones;
- network traffic and storage movement;
- accelerator depreciation and impairment;
- customer prepayments and take-or-pay floors;
- capacity resale and secondary-market prices;
- inference volume and application revenue;
- hardware performance, power and rack cost.

### Writing gate

Every utilization range must identify numerator, denominator, period and triangulation
method. A bounded range requires at least two independent evidence surfaces or one
direct primary measurement. A single indirect observation supports a scenario, not a
measured range.

## Cross-chapter reconciliation

Chapter 12 supplies operating and break-even mechanics. Chapter 13 supplies capital
priority and return by claimant. Chapter 14 supplies utilization, realized price,
useful life and cost-curve inputs. The three chapters must reconcile through stable
project and exposure IDs before any final conclusion is written.
