# Chapter 15 Research Pass: Control and Dependence

## Scope and disposition

This research-only packet maps disclosed governance rights, exclusivity, collateral,
remedies, and platform dependence across the AI financial system. It does not assign
ultimate control where public contracts or charters are incomplete, estimate a control
premium, impute a first-loss waterfall, or treat a financing relationship as ownership.

The governing distinction is four separate questions:

1. **Ownership** asks who holds an equity or legal-title interest.
2. **Contractual control** asks who can direct a defined action through an agreement,
   collateral package, cure right, termination remedy, or exclusivity term.
3. **Accounting control** asks whether the reporting entity has the disclosed power
   over the activities most significant to a VIE or subsidiary's economics.
4. **Operational dependence** asks whether a party's service or economic continuity
   relies materially on a supplier, platform, tenant, customer, or financing channel.

None of the latter three follows automatically from the others. A guarantee can expose
the guarantor without granting board power; a minority investor can be economically
important without controlling a company; and a tenant or manager can be operationally
central while lacking accounting control.

## Observed governance and accounting boundaries

OpenAI Foundation is the clearest disclosed governance-control case: it has exclusive
rights to appoint and replace every OpenAI Group director despite 26 percent economic
ownership (CLM-060001; CLM-020062). Microsoft’s approximately 27 percent economic
interest is not disclosed as board-appointment power. Microsoft’s OpenAI model and
product rights changed from exclusivity in the 2025 agreement to a nonexclusive license
and multi-cloud product permission in the April 2026 amendment (CLM-020059;
CLM-020060). Those are contractual platform and IP rights, not disclosed corporate
governance rights.

Accounting results likewise do not collapse into operational roles. Meta does not
consolidate Hyperion because it lacks power over the venture activities that most affect
economics even though it is a tenant, developer manager, property manager, minority
investor, and residual-value guarantor (CLM-020171; CLM-040024). Conversely Alphabet
consolidated Intersect after determining it was the primary beneficiary, while certain
other Intersect assets remained independently owned (CLM-020087; CLM-050024).

## Contractual rights and lender control

Documented lender control is conditional and instrument-specific. River Bend’s
indenture gives first-priority collateral control over project accounts and substantially
all issuer assets, establishes a revenue priority, and directs a qualifying-replacement
lease process and note repurchase remedy after a lease termination (CLM-040004;
CLM-040005). CCAC VIII has security over borrower assets and pledged equity, while its
CoreWeave parent guarantee is limited to named bad acts rather than ordinary debt
service (CLM-040029). The Ares warehouse documents a cure process, account control,
servicer removal, disposition, and collateral remedies after specified triggers
(CLM-090006). These rights are not general authority over the borrower before their
contractual triggers.

Guarantees should remain separate from collateral control. Alphabet’s recorded maximum
data-center and energy backstops may permit lease assumption, sublease, or termination
payment in some circumstances, but the disclosure does not establish board or operating
control of every supported counterparty (CLM-020082; CLM-070015). The same discipline
applies to Oracle lessor-borrowing support and Google’s River Bend payment backstop.

## Exclusivity and platform dependence

The record supports discrete dependencies, not a universal lock-in measure. OpenAI
remains primarily connected to Azure and has a first-ship condition subject to a stated
Microsoft capability exception, but the current IP license is nonexclusive and OpenAI
may use other clouds (CLM-020060). Amazon’s Anthropic relationship combines investment,
a compute-delivery-gated facility, a long-term AWS commitment, chips, and Bedrock
distribution; the combined dependency reconstruction is Grade C and does not disclose
Amazon governance control (CLM-060008; CLM-020131). CoreWeave’s sole deployed GPU
platform and NVIDIA residual-capacity order demonstrate concentrated operational and
commercial dependence, not ownership or a right to direct CoreWeave (CLM-020019;
CLM-020029).

The packet treats termination rights and nonexclusive terms as falsifiers of simple
lock-in claims. The Anthropic-SpaceX capacity contract can terminate under disclosed
notice mechanics after an initial period, and NVIDIA’s Coherent purchase commitment is
expressly nonexclusive (CLM-060013; CLM-080024).

## Publication boundary

Chapter 15 can state the observed right, holder, target, trigger, scope, and cited
limitation for each ledger row. It can compare the four control dimensions without
equating them. It cannot state an unfiled voting threshold, a complete private-company
board map, an undisclosed collateral schedule, an enforceability outcome, a switching
cost, or a system-wide controller.
