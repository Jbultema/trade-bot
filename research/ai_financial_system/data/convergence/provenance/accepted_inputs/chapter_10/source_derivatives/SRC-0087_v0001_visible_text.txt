Q15. WHAT WOULD BE THE IMPACT ON LOUISIANA UTILITY CUSTOMERS IF 15
LOUISIANA STOPPED INVESTING IN THE TRANSMISSION SYSTEM? 16
A. New load growth would shift to other states , which would lead to lost economic 17
opportunities and ultimately to higher rates for Louisiana utility customers. More 18
specifically, if large load customers elect to site their investments elsewhere, there 19
would be a number of adverse impacts on Louisiana utility customers and the State 20
generally. First, as these new major block loads are sited elsewhere, the interconnected 21
transmission grid and the direct and downstream effects of these large load additions 22
likely would necessitate transmission improvements in Louisiana to maintain reliable 23

Q19. WOULD ELL BE ABLE TO SERVE PROJECT EVEST WITH A TRANSMISSION-4
ONLY SOLUTION? 5
A. No. A transmission-only solution is not a viable option for Project Evest due to the size 6
of the MW load addition. ELL will require additional generation capacity and 7
energy to serve the Customer’s physical load serving needs reliably and to meet ELL’s 8
own planning reserve obligations. Satisfying these requirements and obligations 9
requires additional generation somewhere on the system. Without this additional 10
generation, all ELL customers would be exposed to unreasonable risk with respect to 11
reliability and costs. 5 In addition, the reliability and cost risks associated with a 12
transmission-only solution likely would cause the Customer to eliminate Louisiana as 13
a potential location option for Project Evest. In this scenario, ELL would lose the 14
opportunity to serve the Customer, and Louisiana would lose the substantial direct and 15
secondary economic benefits attributable to industrial development in Richland Parish. 16
17
5 This exposure arises, fundamentally, from tightening capacity supply and demand balances in Louisiana
and MISO South, generally. Tighter supply and demand balances, in turn, are causing clearing prices in
MISO’s Planning Resource Auction (“PRA”) to trend higher. MISO’s implementation of a so- called
“Reliability Based Demand Curve” beginning with the 2025- 2026 Planning Year magnifies the risk of
higher clearing prices in the PRA – and thus the risk of having insufficient capacity resources to cover
ELL’s planning reserve obligations. Further, a lack of adequate transmission investment will lead to
constraints on the transmission system, which will place further upward pressure on energy market costs.

Q20. WHY WOULD ELL NOT BE ABLE TO MEET THE DEMAND NEEDS OF 1
PROJECT EVEST SOLELY BY ADDING GENERATION? 2
A. A generation-only solution for the MW load addition contemplated by Project 3
Evest is not viable due to the size, location, and characteristics of this new load. Project 4
Evest will operate nearly around-the- clock, at a load factor of . Meeting these 5
requirements will require a complex, integrated transmission and generation solution, 6
including several high-capacity factor sources of energy to reliably serve the load while 7
also maintaining the reliability of the bulk electric system and mitigating the cost 8
impacts to ELL customers. The proposed Transmission Facilities and substations are 9
needed to deliver the power to the Customer site and to maintain the reliability of the 10
electric system in Louisiana with the addition of the sizable new load associated with 11
Project Evest and the new generation resources necessary to serve Project Evest. The 12
Transmission Facilities represent a reasonable and cost -effective solution for meeting 13
the transmission requirements for Project Evest. Ms. Beauchamp describes the capacity 14
and energy needs arising from the addition of the Project and the different scenarios 15
considered, and Company witness Norman Grunden provides detailed testimony about 16
the generation facilities that will be constructed to serve the Project. 17

VII. MISO PROCESS AND IMPACT 1
Q100. PLEASE DESCRIBE MISO’S ROLE AND HOW IT AFFECTS THIS PROJECT. 2
A. MISO is the Transmission Planner for the entire MISO region, which extends across 3
all or part of fifteen states in the central United States, including most of Louisiana. As 4
Transmission Planner, MISO a dministers two processes that are pertinent to the 5
Project: the transmission planning process and the generator interconnection process. 6
New substations (such as Smalling) must be studied through the transmission planning 7
process, and new generators must be studied through the generator interconnection 8
process. 9
10
Q101. CAN YOU DESCRIBE THE MISO TRANSMISSION PLANNING PROCESS? 11
A. To connect a new load serving substation to the system, a MISO member must submit 12
a request to MISO. MISO studies that request by adding the proposed substation to its 13
system models and simulating outages of facilities across the system to determine 14
whether the system can support the request and whether any system upgrades are 15
needed to accommodate the new substation reliably. Most such requests are studied 16
annually through MISO’s MTEP process. This process has a deadline in September of 17
each year for the submission of proposed new transmission facilities, including new 18
load serving substations; MISO then evaluates and analyzes the proposed new facilities 19
and decides whether to recommend them for approval by MISO’s Board of Directors . 20
Such approval occurs in December of the year following the year of submission 21
(i.e., approximately 16 months later). 22
23

Q116. CAN YOU DESCRIBE NITS? 15
